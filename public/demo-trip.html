The long read

Filler below the fold so the sidebar can scroll out of view and the floating card can take over. Winter in the Baltics is a study in blue hours: the light arrives late, lingers low, and leaves early, which makes every lit window look like an invitation.

In Vilnius we start slow — cobbles, coffee, and the university courtyards. The old town is compact enough that getting lost is a feature. By afternoon we're out at Trakai, where the castle sits on its island like a set piece nobody struck.

Riga is the architectural main course. Whole streets of art nouveau facades, each doorway more ornate than the last, and a central market housed in old Zeppelin hangars where the smoked fish counter deserves its own itinerary entry.

Then north, into Estonia. Lahemaa's bog boardwalks in winter are silent in a way cities never manage — just wind in the pines and the occasional crack of ice settling. The smoke sauna afterwards is not optional. Well, it is, but it shouldn't be.

Tallinn closes the trilogy: a walled medieval core with a skyline of spires and, in February, a dusting of snow that makes the whole thing look ridiculous, in a good way. Final dinner in a cellar restaurant, toasts all round, and promises to come back in summer to see what all this looks like in the green.
